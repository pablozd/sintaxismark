\section{Purpose and scope}

\pkg{} is a LaTeX package for marking syntactic constituents, grammatical functions, heads, and annotated spans directly over running text. It is designed for teaching materials, exercises, handouts, slides, and compact linguistic analyses where a full syntactic tree would be too heavy.

The experimental monolithic version consists of a single file, \texttt{sintaxismark.sty}. This file contains the classic TikZ-overlay implementation, manual multiline helpers, experimental automatic splitting commands, and the experimental LuaLaTeX grid renderer.

\section{Compilation and installation}

Copy \texttt{sintaxismark.sty} to the same directory as your document, or install it in a local TeX tree.

\begin{lstlisting}
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{sintaxismark}
\begin{document}
Text.
\end{document}
\end{lstlisting}

If you use \texttt{gb4e}, load \texttt{gb4e} first and disable automatic math mode:

\begin{lstlisting}
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{gb4e}
\noautomath
\usepackage{sintaxismark}
\begin{document}
\begin{exe}
\ex \SES{Juan} \PVS{\NV{compró} \OD{manzanas}}.
\end{exe}
\end{document}
\end{lstlisting}

Classic mode uses TikZ with \texttt{remember picture} and \texttt{overlay}. Compile twice if the first run places labels incorrectly. Grid mode and the automatic splitting commands require LuaLaTeX.

\section{Modes of operation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Classic mode}: commands such as \cmd{spanline}, \cmd{openbox}, \cmd{tagbelow}, \cmd{synul}, and the shortcut commands draw annotations directly around the text. This is the most flexible mode for short examples and fine manual control.
  \item \textbf{Manual multiline mode}: helpers such as \cmd{spanfirst}, \cmd{spanmid}, \cmd{spanlast}, \cmd{boxfirst}, \cmd{boxmid}, and \cmd{boxlast} split a long annotation into visually separate segments.
  \item \textbf{Automatic split mode}: \cmd{autospanline} and \cmd{autoopenbox} split plain text automatically. This is experimental and only parses plain text.
  \item \textbf{Grid mode}: the \texttt{sintaxisgrid} environment parses nested sintaxismark markup, computes line breaks, and renders the whole example as one grid. It is intended to avoid diagonal lines in long multiline analyses.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Visual settings inherited from the classic implementation}

The original spacing of \cmd{spanline} and \cmd{openbox} is controlled by the \texttt{/sintaxis} key family. The following defaults are the reference values used by the classic implementation.

\begin{longtable}{>{\ttfamily}p{3.5cm}p{2.2cm}p{8.0cm}}
\toprule
Key & Default & Meaning \\
\midrule
color & black & Annotation color. \\
linew & 0.6pt & Stroke width for spans, boxes, and underlines. \\
liney & 1ex & Vertical length of the legs of upper spans. \\
linegap & 5.5pt & Gap between the text and the lower end of an upper span leg. The upper span line is placed at \texttt{linegap + liney}. \\
labsep & 1ex & Gap between an upper span line and its label. \\
boxd & 3ex & Depth of lower open boxes. \\
boxsep & 1.0ex & Gap between a lower open box and its label. \\
labelsep & unset & General label separation override. If set, it overrides \texttt{boxsep} and \texttt{labelsepboxes}. \\
labelsepboxes & unset & Label separation override for lower boxes. \\
nestedgap & 0.3ex & Extra gap used when nested lower annotations require additional depth. \\
labheight & 1.1ex & Estimated label height used in vertical reservation. \\
tagy & 1.8ex & Vertical distance for floating lower tags. \\
tagx & 0pt & Horizontal displacement for floating lower tags. \\
underw & 0.8pt & Underline stroke width. \\
autogap & 0.6ex & Extra gap used by automatic depth calculation for nested lower boxes. \\
leftend & true & Draw the left vertical end of a span or open box. \\
rightend & true & Draw the right vertical end of a span or open box. \\
autodepth & true & Let nested lower annotations increase the depth of an outer open box. \\
leftarrow & false & Add a left arrow tip to the horizontal line. \\
rightarrow & false & Add a right arrow tip to the horizontal line. \\
arrowlen & 1.3ex & Arrowhead length. \\
arrowtip & Latex & TikZ arrowhead type. \\
arrowscale & 1 & Arrowhead scale. \\
arrowinset & 0pt & Arrowhead inset. \\
labelalign & center & Label alignment for lower boxes: \texttt{left}, \texttt{center}, or \texttt{right}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The compact reservation layer currently reserves a fixed amount of vertical space: \texttt{4.5ex} below the line and \texttt{7.5ex} above the line. This is a pragmatic setting that prevents frequent collisions in examples embedded in \texttt{gb4e}.

\end{lstlisting}

\exampletitle{Changing classic spacing locally for one annotation}

\openbox[boxd=3.6ex,boxsep=1.4ex,linew=1pt]{od}{manzanas}
\tagbelow[tagy=2.2ex,tagx=0.5ex]{md}{las}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \spanline[liney=3ex,linegap=0.8ex,labsep=1.2ex,linew=1pt]{pvs}{compró manzanas}
\ex \openbox[boxd=3.6ex,boxsep=1.4ex,linew=1pt]{od}{manzanas}

\section{Core classic commands}

\subsection{Upper spans: \cmd{spanline}}

\begin{lstlisting}

\end{lstlisting}

\exampletitle{Upper spans}

\spanline[leftend=false,rightarrow,color=red]{pvs}{compró manzanas}

\section{Shortcut commands}

Upper-span shortcuts are wrappers around \cmd{spanline}: \cmd{SES}, \cmd{ST}, \cmd{SEC}, \cmd{PVS}, \cmd{PVC}, and \cmd{OS}.

\exampletitle{Upper shortcut commands}
\begin{lstlisting}
\SES{Juan} \PVS{compró manzanas}
\OS{\SES{Juan} \PVS{compró manzanas}}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \SES{Juan} \PVS{compró manzanas}
\ex \OS{\SES{Juan} \PVS{compró manzanas}}
\end{exe}

Many lower shortcuts are dual commands. Without a star they produce a lower tag; with a star they produce a lower open box.

\begin{lstlisting}
\OD{manzanas}      % lower tag + underline
\OD*{manzanas}     % lower open box
\MI{de Juan}       % lower tag
\MI*{de Juan}      % lower open box
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \OD{manzanas}
\ex \OD*{manzanas}
\ex \MI{de Juan}
\ex \MI*{de Juan}
\end{exe}

\begin{longtable}{>{\ttfamily}p{2.4cm}p{10.9cm}}
\toprule
Command & Output \\
\midrule
SES, ST, SEC, PVS, PVC, OS & Upper spans. \\
OD, OI & Without star: lower label with underline. With star: lower open box. \\
CCL, COMP, MD, MI, CAg, CREG, CSim, CL, CM & Without star: lower tag. With star: lower open box. \\
PSO, PSNO, POO, PONO & Without star: lower tag. With star: lower open box. \\
ACT, ACL, ACM, ACF, ACC, ACCant, ACI, ACFin & Without star: lower tag. With star: lower open box. \\
MG, Ap, MF, IPCR, Incl & Without star: lower tag. With star: lower open box. \\
N, NN, NV, NP, NA, NAD & Simple lower labels; NN and NV include underline. \\
MGV, ApV, MFV, IPCRV, InclV & Lower labels with underline. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\exampletitle{Complex shortcut example}

\PVS{\NV{analizó} \OD*{\MD{una} \NN{oración} \MI*{con \MD{varios} \N{modificadores}}}}

\section{Manual multiline helpers}

If a long span or open box must be split across visual lines, use the manual helpers. They draw separate local segments and avoid diagonal lines.

\begin{lstlisting}
\spanfirst[<keys>]{<label>}{<text>}  % left end, open to the right
\spanmid  [<keys>]{<label>}{<text>}  % continuation, no ends
\spanlast [<keys>]{<label>}{<text>}  % open from the left, right end

\boxfirst[<keys>]{<label>}{<text>}
\boxmid  [<keys>]{<label>}{<text>}
\boxlast [<keys>]{<label>}{<text>}
\end{lstlisting}

\exampletitle{Manual upper split}
\begin{lstlisting}
\spanfirst{pvs}{propuso una consigna extensa}
\spanmid{pvs}{para analizar varios casos}
\spanlast{pvs}{en la clase práctica}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \spanfirst{pvs}{propuso una consigna extensa}\par
    \spanmid{pvs}{para analizar varios casos}\par
    \spanlast{pvs}{en la clase práctica}
\end{exe}

\exampletitle{Manual lower split}
\begin{lstlisting}
\boxfirst{od}{una consigna extensa}
\boxmid{od}{con varios incisos}
\boxlast{od}{para resolver en clase}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \boxfirst{od}{una consigna extensa}\par
    \boxmid{od}{con varios incisos}\par
    \boxlast{od}{para resolver en clase}
\end{exe}

The upper shortcut wrappers are also available: \cmd{SESfirst}, \cmd{SESmid}, \cmd{SESlast}, \cmd{STfirst}, \cmd{STmid}, \cmd{STlast}, \cmd{SECfirst}, \cmd{SECmid}, \cmd{SEClast}, \cmd{PVSfirst}, \cmd{PVSmid}, \cmd{PVSlast}, \cmd{PVCfirst}, \cmd{PVCmid}, \cmd{PVClast}, \cmd{OSfirst}, \cmd{OSmid}, and \cmd{OSlast}.

\section{Line wrapping and no-break variants}

\cmd{SMlinewrap} places its contents in a variable-width paragraph of maximum width \cmd{linewidth}. It allows long examples to wrap naturally.

\begin{lstlisting}
\SMlinewrap{El \MI{departamento de unos monoblocks deteriorados} quedaba abajo.}
\end{lstlisting}

\cmd{openboxNB} and \cmd{spanlineNB} wrap the annotated text in \cmd{mbox}, preventing the marked text from breaking across lines.

\begin{lstlisting}
\openboxNB{mi}{departamento de unos monoblocks deteriorados}
\spanlineNB{ses}{El departamento de unos monoblocks deteriorados}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \SMlinewrap{El \openboxNB{mi}{departamento de unos monoblocks deteriorados} quedaba abajo.}
\end{exe}

\section{The \cmd{breakspan} helper}

\cmd{breakspan} applies a right-opening style to all spans and boxes in its first argument and a left-opening style to all spans and boxes in its second argument.

\begin{lstlisting}
\breakspan
  {\SES{\MI{El departamento de unos monoblocks}}}
  {\SES{\MI{quedaba en la planta baja}}}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex \breakspan
  {\SES{\MI{El departamento de unos monoblocks}}}
  {\SES{\MI{quedaba en la planta baja}}}
\end{exe}

\section{Experimental automatic splitting}

\cmd{autospanline} and \cmd{autoopenbox} require LuaLaTeX. They take plain text, compute a balanced line split, and internally emit the manual multiline helpers.

\begin{lstlisting}
\autospanline[<width>]{<label>}{<plain text>}
\autoopenbox[<width>]{<label>}{<plain text>}
\end{lstlisting}

\exampletitle{Automatic split of an upper span}
\begin{lstlisting}
\autospanline[7cm]{pvs}{propuso una consigna extensa para analizar varios casos en la clase práctica}
\end{lstlisting}

\autospanline[7cm]{pvs}{propuso una consigna extensa para analizar varios casos en la clase práctica}

\exampletitle{Automatic split of a lower box}
\begin{lstlisting}
\autoopenbox[7cm]{od}{una consigna extensa con varios incisos para resolver en la clase práctica}
\end{lstlisting}

\autoopenbox[7cm]{od}{una consigna extensa con varios incisos para resolver en la clase práctica}

These commands are experimental and do not parse nested sintaxismark markup.

\section{Experimental grid mode}

The \texttt{sintaxisgrid} environment reads a full nested structure, extracts tokens, computes line breaks, and draws the whole analysis as a single TikZ picture. It is the preferred mode for long examples in which classic spans or boxes would cross line breaks.

\begin{lstlisting}
\begin{sintaxisgrid}[width=.92\linewidth,valign=base,yoffset=0pt]
\SA{
  \SES{Juan}
  \PVS{
    vio
    \OD*{
      \MD{un}
      \N{tigre}
    }
  }
}
\end{sintaxisgrid}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex
\begin{sintaxisgrid}[width=.92\linewidth,valign=base,yoffset=0pt]
\SA{
  \SES{Juan}
  \PVS{
    vio
    \OD*{
      \MD{un}
      \N{tigre}
    }
  }
}
\end{sintaxisgrid}
\end{exe}

\subsection{Grid interpretation of shortcut commands}

Inside \texttt{sintaxisgrid}, commands are not executed in the classic sense. They are parsed structurally.

\begin{longtable}{>{\ttfamily}p{3.3cm}p{9.6cm}}
\toprule
Command pattern & Grid interpretation \\
\midrule
SES, ST, SEC, PVS, PVC, OS & Upper spans. \\
OD, OI & Lower underlined labels. \\
Any starred shortcut, e.g. OD*, MI*, COMP* & Lower open box. \\
Other unstarred shortcuts, e.g. MD, MI, N, NN, NV, ACL & Lower simple tags. \\
SA & Structural wrapper; it does not draw its own mark. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsection{Grid spacing}

The grid renderer currently uses fixed internal constants tuned to resemble classic \cmd{spanline} and \cmd{openbox}: upper spans approximate \key{liney}/\key{labsep}; lower boxes approximate \key{boxd}/\key{boxsep}; lower tags approximate \key{tagy}; nested lower levels approximate \key{nestedgap} and the classic automatic depth behavior.

\exampletitle{Nested lower marks in grid mode}
\begin{lstlisting}
\begin{sintaxisgrid}[width=.92\linewidth,valign=base]
\SA{
  \SES{\MD{El}\N{estudiante}\MI*{de \MD{la}\N{facultad}}}
  \PVS{analizó \OD*{\MD{una}\N{oración}\MI*{con \MD{varios}\N{modificadores}}}}
}
\end{sintaxisgrid}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex
\begin{sintaxisgrid}[width=.92\linewidth,valign=base]
\SA{
  \SES{\MD{El}\N{estudiante}\MI*{de \MD{la}\N{facultad}}}
  \PVS{analizó \OD*{\MD{una}\N{oración}\MI*{con \MD{varios}\N{modificadores}}}}
}
\end{sintaxisgrid}
\end{exe}

\exampletitle{Forcing a line break by narrowing the grid}
\begin{lstlisting}
\begin{sintaxisgrid}[width=8cm,valign=base]
\SA{
  \SES{\MD{El}\N{departamento}\MI*{de \MD{unos}\N{monoblocks}\MI*{de \MD{color}\N{bordó}}}}
  \PVS{quedaba \ACM{abajo}}
}
\end{sintaxisgrid}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{exe}
\ex
\begin{sintaxisgrid}[width=8cm,valign=base]
\SA{
  \SES{\MD{El}\N{departamento}\MI*{de \MD{unos}\N{monoblocks}\MI*{de \MD{color}\N{bordó}}}}

}
\end{sintaxisgrid}
\end{exe}

The grid renderer emits its generated TikZ code without embedded newline character tokens, so normal use should not produce missing-character warnings.

\section{Recommended workflow}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Use classic mode for short examples and for precise manual control.
  \item Use \cmd{openboxNB}, \cmd{spanlineNB}, or the manual split helpers when an annotated classical span must not cross a line break.
  \item Use \cmd{autospanline} or \cmd{autoopenbox} only for plain text.
  \item Use \texttt{sintaxisgrid} for long examples with nested sintactic markup.
\end{enumerate}
